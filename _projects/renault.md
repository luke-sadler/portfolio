The idea for **Radio Decoder for Renault** came from a personal experience: after replacing the battery in my wife’s Renault Scenic, the radio unit locked itself and required an unlock code to function again. Researching the issue revealed that Renault radios use a precode system, where a short identifier (available via the radio menu or printed on the unit’s label) can be run through an algorithm to generate the required unlock code.  

While the algorithm itself is well-documented online, I noticed that most mobile solutions were either locked behind expensive paywalls or bloated with ad-heavy libraries, making the experience frustrating for users. To solve this, I created a lightweight, free-to-use application that performs the decoding instantly. I converted the algorithm into C++ and integrated it within a Swift iOS project, resulting in a simple, efficient app that puts the solution directly into users’ hands without cost barriers.  

Since launch, the app has been extremely well received, with a 4.9-star rating from over 7,400 reviews and hundreds of thousands of downloads worldwide. Its main user base comes from France, Turkey, the UK, and Brazil, reflecting its broad international appeal. The app stands out for its speed, ease of use, and the value it provides to drivers who would otherwise be locked out of their car’s radio functionality. Many reviews mention it has saved them large amounts of money, avoiding dealership fees.    

What started as a quick solution to a personal problem unexpectedly grew into an app with a global audience. I built it with a focus on **simplicity and efficiency**, stripping away ads, unnecessary features, and heavy assets. As a result, the app is just around **2MB** in size, making it fast to download and accessible even on older devices. Although it was a relatively small project compared to others, the positive reception and widespread use have been especially rewarding, showing how a simple idea can make a big difference for thousands of people.
